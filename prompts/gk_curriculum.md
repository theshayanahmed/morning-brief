# General Knowledge Curriculum

The "stay sharp" half of the daily quiz. Pure recall and reasoning across
broad domains, NOT linked to today's news. Intended to keep Shayan's
general knowledge wide and durable.

The agent picks 5 questions per day across DIFFERENT buckets. Track
coverage in state.gk_progress per bucket: count, last_seen, difficulty (1-4),
rolling_score (last 5).

Selection rules:
• Pick 5 different buckets per session — no two questions from the same
  bucket on the same day
• Prioritise buckets not seen in 7+ days
• Within a bucket, ratchet difficulty over successive sessions
• Every 5th–7th session, throw in one really hard question (difficulty 4)
  in a strong bucket — keeps the ceiling high
• Trivia-grade is fine; obvious is not. The person being tested is a
  well-read senior executive, not a casual quiz-taker

═══════ BUCKETS ═══════

## 1. World history
Ancient civilizations. Empires (Roman, Byzantine, Islamic caliphates,
Mongol, Mughal, Ottoman, British). Renaissance, Reformation, Enlightenment.
Revolutions (American, French, Russian, Chinese, Iranian). World Wars.
Decolonisation. Cold War. Recent history (last 50y). Key figures across
eras. Treaties and turning points.

## 2. Geography
Countries and capitals (esp. less obvious — Burkina Faso, Eswatini,
Suriname). Major rivers, mountain ranges, deserts, oceans. Plate tectonics.
Climate zones. Demographics (most populous, fastest growing, oldest).
Geopolitical features (chokepoints, contested borders, exclaves).

## 3. Physical science
Physics: classical mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, relativity
(special + general), quantum mechanics fundamentals. Chemistry: periodic
table, atomic structure, bonding, organic basics. Earth science: geology,
oceanography, atmosphere, climate.

## 4. Life science
Biology: cell, evolution, genetics (Mendel, DNA, CRISPR), ecology,
physiology basics. Medicine: major discoveries, key figures. Psychology &
neuroscience: foundational experiments, brain regions, cognitive biases.

## 5. Mathematics
Number theory basics. Geometry. Algebra. Calculus concepts (not
computation). Probability and statistics. Famous problems (Fermat, Riemann,
P vs NP). Mathematicians (Euler, Gauss, Ramanujan, Erdős). Logic.

## 6. Literature
Major authors and works across cultures: Western canon (Shakespeare,
Dostoevsky, Tolstoy, Austen, Joyce, Kafka, Hemingway), Latin American
(García Márquez, Borges, Vargas Llosa, Llosa), South Asian (Tagore,
Rushdie, Roy, Lahiri), African (Achebe, Ngũgĩ, Adichie), Russian, French,
German, Japanese (Murakami, Mishima). Major movements (Romanticism,
Modernism, Magical Realism). Poetry. Major literary prizes.

## 7. Visual & performing arts
Painters and movements (Renaissance through contemporary). Architecture
(major styles, key buildings, architects). Sculpture. Photography. Cinema:
foundational directors and films across traditions (Kurosawa, Bergman,
Ray, Kiarostami, Wong Kar-wai, Bong, Scorsese). Theatre.

## 8. Music
Classical (composers, periods — Baroque, Classical, Romantic, 20th-c).
Jazz (key figures, eras). Rock and pop (defining artists across decades).
Hip-hop. World music traditions (Indian classical, qawwali, fado, gamelan,
flamenco). Music theory basics.

## 9. Religion, mythology & philosophy
Major world religions (history, key texts, major branches). Mythologies
(Greek, Roman, Norse, Hindu, Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Yoruba). Philosophy:
Western (Socratic, Stoic, Enlightenment, German Idealism, Existentialism,
Analytic vs Continental); Eastern (Confucian, Daoist, Buddhist, Hindu).
Key thinkers from each tradition.

## 10. Political theory & systems
Political philosophy from Plato/Aristotle through Machiavelli, Hobbes,
Locke, Rousseau, Marx, Weber, Foucault, Rawls. Political systems
(parliamentary vs presidential, federal vs unitary, electoral systems).
Major political thinkers and movements. Constitutional concepts. IR theory
(realism, liberalism, constructivism).

## 11. Economic thought & systems
History of economic thought: classical (Smith, Ricardo, Mill), Marxist,
Marginalist, Keynesian, Austrian (Hayek, Mises), Monetarist (Friedman),
Behavioural (Kahneman, Thaler), Modern (Piketty, Acemoglu). Concepts:
comparative advantage, opportunity cost, externalities, public goods,
Coase theorem, Pareto efficiency, Phillips curve, Laffer curve. Major
crises (1929, 1971, 1997, 2008, 2020).

## 12. Linguistics & language
Language families. Writing systems. Phonetics basics. Major historical
shifts (Great Vowel Shift, Grimm's Law). Etymology of common terms.
Constructed languages. Translation theory. Sociolinguistics. Famous
linguists (Chomsky, Saussure, Sapir-Whorf).

## 13. Sport
Major sports across traditions: cricket (rules, history, key players),
football (World Cup history, major leagues), tennis (Grand Slam history),
F1, golf, NBA/NFL fundamentals, Olympic history. Lesser-touched: kabaddi,
sumo, sepak takraw — for breadth.

## 14. Pop culture & contemporary
Defining films, TV shows, books, albums of recent decades. Major cultural
moments. Internet history (key sites/apps). Defining ad campaigns. Iconic
brands.

═══════ DIFFICULTY GUIDE ═══════

• 1 (basic): something most well-read adults would know
  e.g. "Who wrote War and Peace?" — Tolstoy
• 2 (intermediate): requires above-average reading
  e.g. "Borges or García Márquez — who wrote Ficciones?"
• 3 (advanced): requires real depth in the bucket
  e.g. "What concept connects Borges' 'Garden of Forking Paths' to
  many-worlds quantum interpretation?"
• 4 (edge-case): obscure-but-genuine; for the strong bucket only
